Dr. Kurt Marsden

Neuroscience, Behavior, Zebrafish

In the Marsden lab we aim to better understand the processes underlying neurological disease by uncovering genetic and environmental factors that impact the development of neural circuits and behavior. Using zebrafish as our model system, we take a variety of experimental approaches including CRISPR-mediated genome editing, gene expression analysis, high-throughput behavioral testing, 3- and 4-D analysis of neural circuits, simultaneous imaging of neuronal activity and behavior, and optogenetic techniques. Current projects include studies of auditory and visual processing, epilepsy, CHARGE syndrome, and links between cyanobateria exposures and neurodegeneration.

Dr. Adam Heartstone Rose

Keywords: Anatomy, Evolutionary Biology

In our lab, we study anatomical adaptation predominantly to dietary and locomotive specialization in mammals, especially primates and carnivores. We analyze live animal behavior, muscle fiber architecture, and the shape of bones and teeth often in an effort to deduce the abilities (e.g., diets) of extinct species from their fossil remains. In addition to our anatomical research, we receive funding for projects focused on the effects of participation in STEM activities on students' interest in STEM professions.

Dr. Carlos Goller

Microbiology, Education Research, Mentoring

Dr. Goller focuses on microbiology education and the use of creative thinking and mentoring to improve undergraduate research experiences and digital literacy.

Dr. David Aylor

Genetics, Complex Traits, Genomics

Research focuses on the genetics of complex traits using mouse models. His work incorporates genomics and disease modeling to understand how variation leads to different health outcomes.
